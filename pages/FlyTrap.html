Flytrap

Flytrap is a narrative server simulator taking place over the course of a week at Flytrap, the most popular restaurant in the zone known as The Flytrap. Flies comes from all over to eat here because there are no grocery stores, the food at home sucks, and there are no other restaurants in The Flytrap, except for the Flytrap.

Gameplay

Gameplay is kept relatively simple in Flytrap. All you have to do is put items into a POS machine and then food will come out some time later. It mostly seems to be a manner of planning things ahead so that customers do not lose patience when waiting for their food otherwise, like other restaurant simulator games, the customers will leave. This leaves the actually serving to be rather monotonous, while also adding an element of time-management, just like serving in real-life, to the gameplay. The player also gets a hotbar of up to four items and will have to clear dishes off dirty tables. This all serves to ground the game through its gameplay in such an absurd environment, while also providing somewhat of a time challenge that still requires brain power to focus and make decisions on. The time is also important because tip depends on the timeliness of everything, so the dialogue, which is what was most enjoyable about working in an actual restaurant, is left as something of a scarce reward, a moment of reprieve in the sea of requests and demands and responsibilities.

Dialogue

During dialogue the player is literally locked into dialogue if directly being spoken to, otherwise if the dialogue is something that they overhear, they can simply move on by hitting the same key they did to interact in the first place. Dialogue can only progress at the customer's rate of speaking which puts another strain onto the player's time management gameplay, though past the initial greeting and getting order states little can be demanded by the customer, any conversations past those states have to be initiated by the player.

The dialogue progresses through a state system where each time the player interacts with the guest, if the condition is fulfilled to progress to the next state it will after the dialogue. During the eating state where the player themselves must initiate conversation, if the party is a duo conversation progresses independently of the player, so at whatever point they drop in to overhear the conversation would be in the middle of a word or sentence in order to mimick what it would be like in real life. This also forces the player to dedicate real time to the conversation that could be better spent elsewhere being productive. It's only through potentially sacrificing their own productivity that they are privy to narrative and the "people" that inhabit the world.

Design

The game is called Flytrap because it takes place in a flytrap, and because all the guests are flies. This was mostly inspired by the flytrap that was present at my own place of work, but it also slots quite well narratively into the themes of the game itself. The restaurant is surrounded pretty entirely by a dark void and the restaurant seems like it is in the middle of nowhere. Flies don't live very long. Longevity of life really is all down to perspective. For the common fly living to 40 days would be like a human living to be a centenarian. Some of the dialogue reflects this in flies who are more open to speak of it, otherwise they talk about things to fill the time. Like the weather.

The objective of the game itself is to do good enough at your job that you can pay rent at the end of the week, but that is also where the game ends. I intend for it to be a commentary on the means to the ends that we must go through. It feels like a reality for a lot of people to only know work and nothing else, and the game reflects that. The gameplay isn't particularly fun. About as much fun as matching upturned tiles can be perhaps but it must be done anyways, or you at least will have to die trying.

The dialogue then is detracted by the gameplay which demands good time management and constantly moving from person to person. There is no visible benefit to getting to know these flies, but it is so much more interesting than just serving. The desired outcome then is for their to be a dilemma between the getting to know people, which is the spice of life, or working until the next work week.
