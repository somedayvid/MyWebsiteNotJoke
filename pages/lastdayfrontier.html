Last Day on the Frontier

Last Day on the Frontier is a narrative western game where, you as a lone gunslinger, stumble upon a saloon on the American Frontier. There you encounter various folks all unable to face their sins, leading them to this place where sinners wait to die. Duel with a distinct cast of sinners under the clocktower, as you wait for the Frontier to die.

Gameplay

Gameplay in Last Day on the Frontier is focused on the dialogue as well as the singular action of pulling the trigger. The player makes their decisions and lives and dies by whether they decide to shoot or not, on the frontier. Some gunfighters shoot faster, others not at all, it is up to the player to pay careful attention to the dialogue in order to make their decision before they pull the trigger. A duel is more complicated than that though. Like any good ol-fashioned duel, the player can cheat in their duel, setting them down a certain path in their interactions with other characters in the story.

There are many hidden choices the player can make throughout the game which affect their path in the story as well as changing dialogue and story events. I wanted to put emphasize on the hidden choices that players make which in the end have no totaling effect on the story and what happens to the place in the end. As such the finale is always the same: the Frontier dies, and all its inhabitants with it.

Dialogue

Dialogue revolves mainly around the recurring cast of saloon employees and the duelist of the day. Everyday brings another sinner which the player can choose to interact with. Events are triggered through dialogue so choosing to not speak is a choice as well, just one that leads the player down another path, despite that the end result is the same.

Characters

There are 7 gunfighters in the game, each of which represent a deadly sin that they are unable to overcome, and the reason that they cannot ever leave the frontier, and why they are drawn here to the last of the Frontier. Though each of them individually have very little screentime, they all are evidently running from something which is communicated through their dialogue and their duel. Characters also interact with you differently based on the consequences of previous duels.

Design

One physical consideration I took when choosing which buttons would be mapped to which actions is where the shoot button for duels would go, for which I settled eventually on the spacebar for its size compared to any other key on the keyboard. It is physically the largest and makes a different, thunkier sound when pressed, yet it is just as easy as any other button on the keyboard to press. The physicality of the button itself as something that a frequent computer user uses often, turned into the trigger for death is an interesting twist, and has led to many moments of me shooting my opponent before I myself meant to, if I was.
